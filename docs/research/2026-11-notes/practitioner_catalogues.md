# Practitioner and community catalogues of LLM writing tells (2025-2026), gaps vs slop-lint 0.9.1

Scope note. Coverage baseline read on 2026-11-25: `slop-lint.mjs` 0.9.1 (WORD_GROUPS, PHRASES, EMOJI, DOUBLEDASH, SMARTQUOTE), the `no-ai-tells` SKILL.md (catalogue 0.9.0), and `corpus/specimens/PENDING.md`. Everything below is reported only if slop-lint does NOT already catch it with a line rule (some items are in the skill's R5 "check by reading" table but have no regex; those are marked "skill-only").

Counting convention. "Sightings" = distinct authors/organizations reporting the tell. Several GitHub skills (blader/humanizer, bjcoombs/deslop, forjd/better-writing, devstuff fork) state that they are built on Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing, so they are NOT counted as independent of Wikipedia for items Wikipedia already lists. Jim Bennett's Arize post and his jimbobbennett.dev post are the same study (one sighting). ## Q1. What does the current Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" page list, and what does slop-lint not cover?

### Takeaway
The page (revision 1387897756, saved 2026-11-25 22:43 UTC) has moved em-dash overuse to "Historical indicators (2022-September 2026)" and promotional puffery to "Historical (2023-2025)"; its current emphasis is on content-level molds (notability/attribution padding, "identifies him as", mid-2026 "should be treated as X rather than Y" disclaimers, vague "associated with"), a new GPT-6-era vocabulary list, and unambiguous citation/markup artifacts (utm_source, oaicite, turn0search0, Gemini `[cite: N]`/`start_span`, Grok `grok-card`). Most of these are uncovered by slop-lint and many are regexable with near-zero false-positive risk.

### Cited Findings
Source for every item in this section unless noted: [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing, rev 1387897756, 2026-11-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) (WikiProject AI Cleanup, many editors; raw wikitext fetched via `action=raw`), plus subpages [Signs of AI writing/Markup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Markup), [Signs of AI writing/Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing/Style) and [Signs of AI use in comments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_use_in_comments).

**A. Status changes that affect slop-lint's severity model**
- Em dash is now a historical indicator: section retitled "Overuse of em dashes (2022-September 2026)". It says GPT-5.1 was tuned to suppress them, cites "a July 2026 study" finding only Claude used em dashes more than professional writers, and says the newest models (Sept 2026 on) are "hard coded" not to use them, with the side effect that "commas and periods are more likely now than even human text" (that last claim is tagged [citation needed], Oct 2026). Corroborated: The Economist, 2026-07-30, 55,940 sentences / 1.2m words, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok: "Today only Claude uses more em-dashes than human writers, with ChatGPT using markedly fewer" ([Daring Fireball quoting The Economist, 2026-08-11](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/11/economist-ai-writing); [Dataconomy, 2026-08-04](https://dataconomy.com/2026/08/04/how-to-spot-ai-writing)). Jim Bennett measured Claude Opus 5 at 12.9 em dashes per 1,000 words and Opus 5.5 at two in 57,000 words ([Arize, 2026-09-29](https://arize.com/blog/anthropic-says-it-fixed-claudes-writing/)). Wikipedia also notes AI em dashes are "usually surrounded by spaces".
- Promotional language ("boasts a", "nestled", "in the heart of", "natural beauty", "commitment to", "breathtaking") moved to "Historical (2023-2025)": "no longer common in newer LLM output unless a user specifically asks for it."
- Knowledge-cutoff statements ("as of my last knowledge update", "up to my last training update") are historical (2022-2023). Prompt refusal ("As an AI language model") historical. Section summaries ("In summary", "In conclusion", "Overall") historical but "still observed after 2024, just much less."
- "Ineffective indicators" explicitly lists: perfect grammar, mixed registers, "bland" prose, "fancy" prose, transition words in isolation ("Additionally", "Consequently", "Notably" called "not a strong tell"), unsourced content. Relevant to slop-lint's `furthermore/moreover/additionally/notably` group.

**B. AI vocabulary by era (Words to watch box + era list)**
- Box: Additionally, align with, boasts, bolstered, crucial, deep dive, delve, emphasizing, enduring, enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/intricacies, landscape, meticulous, pivotal, robust, showcase, tapestry, testament, underscore, valuable, vibrant. Uncovered by slop-lint WORDS: **emphasizing, enduring, highlight/highlighting (verb), interplay, intricacies, valuable** (only "valuable insights" phrase), **crucial** and **align with** (deliberately rejected in 0.3.0 for FP), **fostering/fostered** (only "foster" is listed; reWord is whole-word so "fostering" does not match "foster").
- Era list: 2023-mid-2024 (GPT-4): adds "key", "interplay". Mid-2024 to mid-2025 (GPT-4o): align with, fostering, highlighting, showcasing. Mid-2025 to mid-2026 (GPT-5): emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing. **Mid-2026 on (GPT-6): challenge, clearer, dependable, echoed, foster, leverage, matters, multifaceted, practical, prioritize, quietly, steady, universally**, "with much more period and comma usage and shorter sentences around a strict 8-20 word limit." The page flags "There are no examples given for 2026 AI vocabulary" (so the GPT-6 list is not yet example-backed on-wiki).
- Grok: "superficially scientific" words **causal, empirical, correlate**, and still "underscore" as of 2026.
- Inflection gap in slop-lint: "underscored", "showcased", "garnered", "garnering", "fostered", "fostering", "elevated", "elevating", "harnessed", "leveraged", "unlocked", "navigated", "delved" are not in WORDS (reWord matches whole words only). Regex sketch for a stem rule: `\b(underscor|showcas|garner|foster|elevat|harness|leverag|delv)(e|es|ed|ing)?\b`.

**C. Content molds (all uncovered unless noted)**
- Undue significance/legacy (WP:AILEGACY). Words to watch: "is a ... reminder", "underscores/highlights its importance/significance", "reflects broader", "symbolizing its ongoing/enduring/lasting", "contributing to the", "marking/shaping the", "represents/marks a shift", "key turning point", "evolving landscape", "focal point", "forms part of the wider", "distinction [matters/is important] because". 2026 examples: "forms part of the wider history" (Oct 2026), "reflected the growing influence" (Oct 2026), "providing evidence of the ... significance" (Nov 2026), "reflects the importance of the location as" (Aug 2026). slop-lint covers only "setting the stage for", "indelible mark", "deeply rooted in", "is a testament to". Regex: `\b(marking|marks|marked|represent(s|ed|ing)?) an? (pivotal|significant|key|major|important) (moment|shift|turning point|milestone)\b`; `\b(reflect(s|ed|ing)?|underscor\w+|highlight\w*) (the )?(broader|wider|growing|enduring|lasting|ongoing) \w+`; `\bforms? part of (the|a) (wider|broader)\b`; `\b(enduring|lasting) (legacy|influence|relevance|significance)\b`.
- "Has shaped discussions about" (WP:ITSHAPESDISCUSSIONS), often with a triad: "has generated debate about authenticity, consent, and ...", "shaped emerging policy discussions about", "contributed to wider discussions about" (Oct 2025, Nov 2026). Regex: `\b(shap(ed|ing)|generat(ed|ing)|prompt(ed|ing)|contribut(ed|ing) to|sparked|fuel(led|ing)) (broader |wider |ongoing |emerging |public |policy )*(discussions?|debates?|conversations?|reflection) (about|on|around|regarding)\b`.
- Notability/attribution padding (WP:AIATTR), "most common in text from AI tools released from mid-2025 to mid-2026": "independent coverage", "local/regional/national media outlets", "trade publications", "cited/featured/profiled in", "has been extensively documented across major national and regional media networks, including ..." (Nov 2026). Regex: `\b(has|have) been (featured|profiled|cited|documented|mentioned|covered) (in|across|by)\b`; `\b(independent|regional|national|international) (media )?coverage\b`; `\b(prominent |major )?(media|news) outlets\b`.
- Identification by primary sources (WP:AIIDENTIFY), "AI-generated text from 2026 on": "His personal website identifies him as", "His parliamentary biography records that", "databases/records/listings identify ... as", "is listed in", "his/her professional profile lists". Regex: `\b(website|biography|profile|records?|database|listing|filings?|materials?) (identif(y|ies)|lists?|records?|describes?) (him|her|them|it|the \w+) as\b|\bwas identified by [^.]{1,40} as\b`.
- Online presence: "maintains an active/strong digital presence", "maintains a social media presence" (June 2025; two Nov 2026 examples). Regex: `\bmaintains? an? (active |strong |robust |growing )?(digital|online|social media) presence\b`.
- Superficial -ing tails (WP:SUPERFICIAL): skill-only (R5.2); no lint regex. Regex sketch (high FP, needs density threshold): `,\s+(highlighting|underscoring|emphasizing|reflecting|symbolizing|showcasing|demonstrating|illustrating|cementing|solidifying|ensuring|fostering|contributing to)\b[^.]{5,}\.$`. New 2026 example family: RAG models attach the -ing analysis to a named source ("Roger Ebert highlighted the lasting influence").
- Vague connection (WP:AICONNECT): "in connection with", "associated with", "connected to", examples Mar-Jul 2026. Too common for a line rule; density statistic only.
- Vague attribution / overgeneralization (WP:AIWEASEL): "Industry reports", "Observers have cited", "Experts argue", "Some critics argue", "described in scholarship", "modern researchers treat", "researchers and conservationists". Skill-only (R5.4). Regex: `\b(experts|observers|critics|analysts|scholars|researchers|commentators) (argue|note|have (noted|cited|argued)|suggest|point out)\b|\b(industry|toy industry|trade) (reports|publications) (such as|suggest|indicate)\b|\bdescribed in (the )?scholarship\b`.
- Outline-like challenges/future conclusions (WP:FACESCHALLENGES): "Despite its [positive words], X faces (several) challenges", "Despite these challenges", headings "Challenges and Legacy", "Future Outlook", "Challenges and Future Directions"; still seen July 2026. Regex: `\bdespite (its|their|these|the) [^,.]{0,60}(challenges|setbacks|limitations)?,? [^.]{0,60}\bfaces? (several |many |numerous |significant |new )?challenges\b`; `\bdespite these challenges\b`; heading `^#{1,6}\s*(challenges|future (outlook|prospects|directions))\b`.
- "Awards and recognition" section: "nearly ubiquitous in AI generated articles", plus general "X and Y" headings. Regex (markdown heading): `^#{1,6}\s*(awards and recognition|recognition|legacy and impact|challenges and \w+)\s*$`.
- Copula avoidance (WP:AINOCOPULA): "serves as/stands as/marks/functions as/operates as/represents [a]", "boasts/features/maintains/offers [a]", "refers to" in leads; observed in GPT and Gemini. slop-lint covers only "serves as a powerful/valuable/vital tool" and "stands/serves as a testament". Regex: `\b(serves|stands|functions|operates) as (a|an|the)\b`; `\bholds the distinction of\b`.
- Negative parallelism variants: "no ..., no ..., just ..." and the reversed "Y rather than X", called "particularly common in recent LLM output". slop-lint's "as an X rather than a Y" requires articles. Regex for bare rather-than: `\b(should|must|can|is|are) (be )?(treated|read|understood|seen|viewed|presented|regarded) as [\w -]{1,40} rather than\b`.
- Mid-2026 "corrective framing" disclaimers (WP:AIDISCLAIMER), said to be "extremely uncommon, verging on unheard-of" before the July 2026 GPT-5.6 release: (1) "[claim] should be treated/presented as X rather than Y"; (2) "does not by itself establish"; "Although the numerical figure should not be treated as an exact census..."; (3) "should not be confused with" (flagged as less reliable). Older (2024-2025) RAG form: "While specific details are limited/scarce", "not widely available/documented/disclosed", "in the provided/available sources/search results", "based on available information". Regex: `\bdoes not (by itself|on its own|alone) (establish|prove|confirm|demonstrate)\b`; `\bshould not be (treated|read|taken) as (an? )?(exact|definitive|conclusive|literal)\b`; `\bwhile (specific )?(details|information) (is|are) (limited|scarce)\b`; `\bnot widely (available|documented|disclosed|reported)\b`; `\bin the (provided|available) (sources|search results|materials)\b`; `\bbased on (the )?available information\b`.
- Leads treating list/broad titles as proper nouns ("X refers to ..."); not regexable cheaply.
- Rule of three: skill-only (R5.1). Wikipedia notes it is stronger in contexts like edit summaries.

**D. Communication intended for the user (WP:COLLABCOMM)**
- Words to watch: "a Wikipedia-style article", "I hope this helps" (covered), "Of course!", "Certainly!", "You're absolutely right!" (covered), "Would you like...", "is there anything else", "let me know", "more detailed breakdown", "here is a ...", "[check/verify/confirm] ... before [publishing/submitting]". Uncovered regexes: `^\s*(certainly|of course|absolutely|sure)!`; `\bwould you like (me to|a|an|the)\b`; `\b(is there )?anything else (i can|you'?d like)\b`; `\blet me know if you('d| would)? (like|want|need|have)\b`; `\b(more detailed|step-by-step) breakdown\b`; `\b(verify|check|confirm|double-check) [^.]{0,60}before (publishing|publication|submitting|posting)\b`; `\bhere(?: is|'s) an? (revised|updated|polished|rewritten|draft|version)\b`.
- Placeholders (WP:AIPLACEHOLDER): Mad-Libs brackets, "INSERT_SOURCE_URL_##", "INSERT_WEBSITE_URL_HERE", date "2025-xx-xx", `example.com` in citations, letter templates "I hope this message finds you well. I am writing to request ...", "Thank you for your understanding and assistance". Regex: `\[(your|insert|company|name|recipient|date|link|title)[^\]]{0,30}\]`; `\bINSERT_[A-Z_]+\b` (case-sensitive); `\b20\d\d-(xx|XX)-(xx|XX)\b`; `\bexample\.com/(article|news|source\d*)\b`.

**E. Markup and citation artifacts (unambiguous; all uncovered)**
- ChatGPT: `:contentReference[oaicite:N]{index=N}`, `oai_citation`, `[oai_citation:N‡domain](url)`, `Name+1` / `IT Governance+3ISO+3ISO+3` link-chip residue, `citeturn0search0`, `turn0image0`, `citeturn0news0`, `citeturn1file0` "surrounded by Unicode points in the Private Use Area", and JSON tails `({"attribution":{"attributableIndex":"1009-1"}})`. Regex: `contentReference\[oaicite:\d+\]|\boai_citation\b|\b(cite)?turn\d+(search|image|news|file|view)\d+\b|"attributableIndex"|[-]`; link-chip `\b[A-Z][\w .]{1,30}\+\d\b` (FP risk with "C+1"; use only adjacent to a period or link).
- URL params (WP:UTMSOURCE): `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, `utm_source=openai`, `utm_source=copilot.com`, `referrer=grok.com`; Gemini/Claude "less often". Regex: `[?&](utm_source=(chatgpt\.com|openai|copilot\.com|perplexity)|referrer=grok\.com)\b`. Note Wikipedia: proves ChatGPT found the citation, not that it wrote the prose.
- Gemini: `[cite: 17]`, `[cite: 19, 20]`, `[span_2](start_span)` / `[span_2](end_span)`, and `<mark>...</mark>` wrappers from "Double-check response" (examples May and Oct 2026). Regex: `\[cite:\s*\d+(,\s*\d+)*\]|\[span_\d+\]\((start|end)_span\)|<mark>`.
- Grok: `<grok-card data-id=... data-type="citation_card">`, `grok_render_citation_card_json`. Regex: `grok[-_](card|render_citation_card_json)`.
- DeepSeek: lenticular brackets and dagger symbols (subpage heading "DeepSeek: lenticular brackets, dagger symbols"; e.g. CJK brackets U+3010/U+3011 with a dagger U+2020). Regex: `【[^】]{0,40}†[^】]{0,40}】` (I did not read the example body; pattern inferred from heading, verify).
- Perplexity: `attached_file`, `ppl-ai-file-upload`. Regex: `\b(attached_file|ppl-ai-file-upload)\b`. Unclassified: `:::writing` fences.
- Markdown in a non-markdown target: `**bold**` asterisks, `#` headings, `---`/`----` thematic breaks between sections, `[text](url)` links, whole text in code fences. For slop-lint (which lints markdown) only the plaintext variant applies: in `.txt` files, `\*\*[^*]+\*\*` or `^#{1,6} ` or `^-{3,}$`.
- Style subpage: Title Case section titles, bold overuse and "inline-header vertical lists" (bold label + colon), wrong heading levels (H1 article title, multiple H1s, headings containing only headings), emoji at start of headings/list items (emoji already covered), unusual tables, curly quotes (covered).

**F. Comment and edit-summary register (Wikipedia talk/AfC; transferable to support tickets, forum replies, PR comments)**
- Edit-summary formulae: "ensured that ... adheres to", "refined", "enhanced", "enriched", "improved clarity and flow", "neutral tone", "encyclopedic tone", "preserved/retained ... information", "avoided ...", "added sourced/verified content", "with independent/secondary/peer-reviewed sources". Directly analogous to AI commit messages ("Improved clarity and flow; preserved existing behavior"). Regex: `\b(preserv|retain)(ed|ing) (all |the )?(existing|original|key)\b|\bimproved (clarity|readability|flow|structure)( and (flow|readability|clarity))?\b|\bensur(ed|ing) (that )?[^.]{0,40}(adheres|complies|aligns) (to|with)\b`.
- Comment words: "I understand your concern(s) regarding", "I understand the importance of", "I respectfully request / respectfully contest", "concrete evidence/examples", "I am happy to / I'd be happy to", "A, particularly B" ("guidelines, particularly the ones on notability"), letterlike "Dear [user], I hope this message finds you well", "Thank you for your time and consideration", "If there are specific issues ... please point them out", apologies "for any inconvenience I may have caused". Regex: `\bI understand (your concerns?|the importance of|that [^.]{0,40}(policies|guidelines|rules))\b`; `\b(I|we) respectfully (request|contest|ask|submit)\b`; `\bconcrete (evidence|examples)\b`; `\b(I am|I'm|I would be|I'd be|I will be) (more than )?happy to\b`; `\bif there are (any )?specific (issues|concerns|passages|changes)\b`; `\bfor any inconvenience (this|I) (may have|might have|has)\b`.
- Claude-specific disclosure: "I used Claude (Anthropic) to ...", "written with Claude, an AI assistant (by Anthropic)" (two Nov 2026 examples). Regex: `\bClaude \(Anthropic\)|\bClaude, an AI assistant\b`.

### Inferences
- The strongest new line rules for slop-lint are the artifact family (E): oaicite/turn0search0/utm_source/`[cite: N]`/start_span/grok-card/PUA. They are single-source (Wikipedia) but the bar for a structural *mold* (two sightings) arguably should not apply to machine-generated tokens that humans never type; they could ship as a separate "artifact" group, possibly at FAIL severity.
- The em-dash FAIL is now contested by the field's own reference page and by The Economist. slop-lint's choice to FAIL on U+2014 remains defensible as a house style rule, but the README/CHANGELOG should stop calling it "near-decisive" as an authorship signal for current models (it now mostly fingerprints Claude Opus 5 and earlier, and older ChatGPT).
- Several Wikipedia molds (AIIDENTIFY, online presence, notability padding, mid-2026 disclaimers) are Wikipedia-register specific, but the disclaimer family ("does not by itself establish", "should be treated as X rather than Y") will likely appear in any GPT-5.6+/GPT-6 research or docs output.

- The "July 2026 study" cited for em dashes is not named in the fetched text (likely The Economist, but not confirmed from the page).

## Q2. What tells have writers and developers reported for GPT-5.x, Claude 4.x/5.x, Gemini 2.5/3 in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
The 2025-26 consensus has shifted from formal vocabulary ("delve") to a casual "founder on LinkedIn" register: sincerity adverbs (quietly, genuinely, honestly, actually), stock mechanical metaphors (load-bearing, earns its keep, does the heavy lifting), salience flags ("This matters."), verdict intensifiers ("the honest answer", "the whole point"), suspense signposts ("Here's the kicker", "Here's the part that..."), staccato countdowns ("Not X. Not Y. Z."), self-answered fragment questions ("The result? ..."), and LinkedIn broetry. Most of these have 3+ independent sightings and are uncovered by slop-lint.

**Layout / rhythm tells (document-level, not single-line regex)**
- Broetry: one sentence per paragraph, blank line between, dramatic hook line. Sightings 9: S14, S16, S17 ("People write in bursts, models write in rows"), S18, S20, S21 (#9, #10 "Like. S16 also reports the opposite older failure: uniform 3-4 sentence paragraphs. Detection sketch: ratio of paragraphs with 1 sentence and fewer than 12 words over total paragraphs > 0.6 with at least 6 paragraphs.
- Punctuation scarcity and "and" overuse (Economist): fewer commas and semicolons, "hardly any parentheses", longer sentences, "and" the most overused word across all four models. Sightings: S25 (primary), S6 (cites S25), [tinytechguides 2026-08-21](https://tinytechguides.com/blog/how-to-spot-ai-content-transitions/) (cites S25). Effectively 1 primary. Contradicted in part by S1, which says GPT-6-era output has "much more period and comma usage and shorter sentences around a strict 8-20 word limit", and by S1's [citation needed] claim that post-Sept-2026 models overuse commas and periods. Likely a model/time split (Economist corpus mid-2026 vs GPT-6). Fits `corpus-stats.mjs`, not a line rule.
- "Contrast starvation": almost no coordinating "but" in a long piece (S6 only).
- Anaphora abuse ("It's about X. It's about Y. It's about Z.") S6, S7: 2 sightings. Regex: `\b(it'?s about|this is|we need) [^.?!]{1,40}[.!]\s+\1 [^.?!]{1,40}[.!]\s+\1` (case-insensitive backreference works in JS with `i`).
- Listicle in a trench coat ("The first... The second... The third...") S7 only.
- Four-beat arc (claim, expand, token complication "that said", tidy resolution) S16 only, citing unnamed "corpus research".
- Model-specific reports: Claude Opus 5 writes ~3x longer than Opus 4.5 (510 vs 158 words/response), sentences 58% longer (S10). Opus 5.5 vs Opus 5: em dash nearly gone, Claudisms halved (4.79 to 2.38 per 1k), but 2.5x more bold lead-in bullets, 35% more three-part lists, 8% longer (S9). ChatGPT and Claude produce the most "not X but Y" scaffolds and rule-of-three triplets per 1,000 sentences (S25 via S6). GPT-5.x reintroduced "Good question"/"Great start" openers and uses curly quotes (S24, second-hand, unverified).

## Q3. Code and developer registers: commit messages, PR descriptions, code comments, READMEs

### Takeaway
Developer complaints converge on (1) comments that narrate the obvious, (2) chat leakage into files ("Here's the updated...", "as requested", "I've added"), (3) tutorial scaffolding ("Step 1:", "Example usage:", "// rest of your code here"), (4) diff-anchored history comments ("This function was added to replace..."), and (5) PR descriptions that are comprehensive "what" summaries with emoji, tables and boilerplate testing claims but no "why". slop-lint covers none of the code-comment patterns and only incidentally catches PR prose via "comprehensive"/"robust".

- No corpus study of AI commit-message wording was found; the ACM study (C10) covers PR description faithfulness, not style.

## Q4. Email and business registers

### Takeaway
Email-tell guides from 2026 agree on a small, regexable set: "I hope this email/message finds you well", "I wanted to reach out / I'm reaching out", "Please don't hesitate to reach out", "I look forward to hearing from you", "quick 15-minute call", "I came across your profile", and Claude's "I'd be happy to help". ## Q5. Unicode and formatting artifacts beyond em-dash and curly quotes

### Takeaway
The best-documented invisible artifact is U+202F narrow no-break space in OpenAI o3/o4-mini output (April 2025, reported by Rumi, gone within days per OpenAI), with later developer reports in GPT-5 output; U+00A0 arrives via chat-UI HTML on copy. Zero-width characters are mostly pipeline artifacts, not model output, and nobody credible treats them as authorship evidence. Visible formatting tells with 2+ sightings: bold-label bullets, Title Case headings, colon-subtitle headings, "→" arrows (already caught by EMOJI), horizontal rules between sections, emoji bullets (caught), and ChatGPT's PUA citation characters (Q1).

- Bold lead-in bullets is the strongest formatting candidate (4 sightings, rising in Opus 5.5 per S9) and is regexable in markdown, slop-lint's default target.

## Q6. What do humanizer tools and anti-slop prompts target (proxy for practitioner consensus)?

### Takeaway
The most-starred humanizer skill (blader/humanizer v4, Nov 2026) has reorganized around "staging instead of stating" (not-X-but-Y, one-line closers, sayings that sound deep, staged run-ups like "Honestly?" and "The hard part is...", arguing with no one), with vocabulary demoted because "word habits change with every model release; the structural habits above persist." Anthropic itself now ships an anti-pattern ("mannered prose") in the Fable 5.1 prompting guide. Shared ban lists add a stable 2025-26 tail: quietly, genuinely, load-bearing, "full stop", "worth stating plainly", "carry the argument". Several of the humanizer's v4 patterns are not in slop-lint.

### Cited Findings
- blader/humanizer "five strongest tells": Not X but Y; one-line closers ("Let that sink in."); sayings that sound deep ("At its core, what really matters is...", "Symmetry is the language of trust"); staged run-up ("Here's the thing." "Honestly?" "The hard part is..."); arguing with no one ("I'm not saying X, but...", "This isn't mainly about...", "A tempting approach would be..."): [blader/humanizer, 2026-01-18, updated 2026-11-25](https://github.com/blader/humanizer).
