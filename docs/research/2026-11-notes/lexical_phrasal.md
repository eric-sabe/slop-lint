# Empirically measured LLM lexical and phrasal tells (2023 to Nov 2026), beyond the slop-lint 0.9.1 catalogue

Scope note. Coverage baseline was read from `slop-lint.mjs` (VERSION 0.9.1, WORD_GROUPS and PHRASES), the no-ai-tells SKILL.md (catalogue 0.9.0), and `corpus/specimens/PENDING.md`. Only items NOT already covered are listed as candidates, plus retire/reword evidence. Research date: 2026-11-25.

A coverage fact found while diffing, which matters for every word list below: `reWord()` builds `\b<word>\b`, so the catalogue matches exact forms only. Many inflections of already-catalogued stems are therefore not flagged today. A script diff against the catalogue found these missing: delved, showcased, underscored, elevates, elevating, elevated, fosters, fostering, fostered, garnered, garnering, garners, leverages, leveraged, harnesses, harnessing, harnessed, empowers, empowering, empowered, unlocking, unlocks, unleashing, revolutionizing, revolutionized, realms, encompass, encompasses, encompassed, surpassed, surpassing, illuminates, illuminating, utilizes, utilizing, utilized, boasting, boasted, exemplify, exemplified, exemplifying, navigates, navigated, testaments, facets, tapestries, intricacies, commendably, journeys, landscapes, ecosystems. Several of these (delved, showcased, underscored, fostering, fosters, garnered, garnering, elevates, elevating, leverages, harnesses, harnessing, empowers, unlocking, revolutionizing, realms, encompass, encompasses, encompassed, surpassed, surpassing, illuminates, illuminating, utilizes, intricacies) are themselves on the Kobak et al. measured excess list (Q1), so this is the cheapest high-evidence gain available.

## Q1. Excess-vocabulary studies (academic register): what they measured and which words are missing

### Takeaway
The biomedical and arXiv excess-vocabulary studies supply about 250 measured style words that slop-lint does not cover, but most are ordinary academic verbs with high false-positive risk. The usable subset is (a) missing inflections of catalogued stems, (b) a short list of rare, ornate words (elucidate, unveil, intricacies, burgeoning, unparalleled, groundwork, uncharted, underexplored, interplay, nuanced, pinpoint, poised, akin, amidst, adept, formidable, pioneering, surmount, swiftly, commendable-family adverbs from peer reviews), and (c) the Liang peer-review adverbs (lucidly, aptly, methodically, innovatively, compellingly, impressively, excellently).

### Cited Findings

Published Science Advances 11(27), 2 Jul 2025 (arXiv v5 3 Jul 2025). Era: GPT-3.5/GPT-4 (2023 to 2024 data). Source: [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016)

By frequency gap delta: "potential" 0.052, "findings" 0.041, "crucial" 0.037. 379 excess style words in 2024 (66% verbs, 14% adjectives); 454 excess words overall. Source: [arXiv 2406.07016v5 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5)

- Hubarava & Ineichen (EMNLP 2026, arXiv 19 Oct 2026), interrupted time series on arXiv (AI, stats, q-bio) and medRxiv: some lexical markers accelerate persistently while others rise and recede; also longer words, lower readability, shallower dependency trees. No word list in the abstract. Source: [arXiv 2610.26008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26008)

### Inferences
- Highest-value, lowest-FP adds from this question: the missing inflections listed in the scope note; then rare ornate words that ordinary academic prose seldom needs: elucidate, elucidates, elucidating, unveil, unveils, unveiled, unveiling, intricacies, burgeoning, unparalleled, uncharted, underexplored, groundwork, interplay, surmount, juxtaposed, interconnectedness, poised, akin, amidst, adept, potent, commendably, lucidly, aptly, methodically, innovatively, compellingly, impressively, excellently. FP risk: moderate for interplay, nuanced, akin, poised, potent (common in humanities and pharmacology); low for lucidly, innovatively, excellently, compellingly, commendably.
- Words like potential, findings, crucial, significant, these, enhance, capabilities are measured excess words but common in human academic prose. They work only as corpus-level statistics (density), not line rules; the 0.3.0 rejection of "crucial" is consistent with this.
- Kobak 2024 numbers are GPT-3.5/GPT-4-era. The 2025 to 2026 follow-ups reuse the 2024 marker set and do not publish a refreshed per-word list, so no study found gives GPT-4o/GPT-5-era academic marker words by name.

### Gaps
- The 21 Juzek & Ward focal words: PDF did not extract; only delve, intricate, underscore confirmed.
- Matsui and Gray ("meticulously commendable") were not retrieved in this pass; no numbers recorded.
- No per-word 2025 or 2026 English excess ranking found (the PMC follow-up publishes aggregates only; its code is at github.com/kobaklab/llm-usage-in-pmc and was not checked).

## Q2. Creative-writing slop lists and model fingerprinting

### Takeaway
Creative-writing slop is a different vocabulary (physical-reaction cliches, invented fantasy names, dialogue tags) with ratios in the hundreds to tens of thousands. None of it is in slop-lint, which is tuned for expository and reply prose. A small set transfers to general prose (palpable, newfound, unspoken, fleeting, etched, testament-to-human style phrases); the rest belongs in an optional fiction profile.

### Cited Findings
- Paech et al., "Antislop" (arXiv 2510.15061v2, 21 Oct 2025). Corpus ~2,000 creative outputs per model from Reddit writing prompts; human baseline wordfreq (words) and Reddit creative writing plus Gutenberg (n-grams). gemma-3-12b word ratios vs human: "elara" 85,513x, "unsettlingly" 3,833x, "shimmered" 2,882x, "stammered" 2,043x. Trigrams (stopwords removed): "heart hammered ribs" 1,192x, "voice trembling slightly" 731x, "said voice devoid" 693x, "felt profound sense" 550x. "It's not X, it's Y" ~6.3x human in some models. Source: [arXiv 2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15061)
- Same paper, share of 67 models with the item in their top list: "flickered" 98.5%, "flicker" 94.0%, "flickering" 92.5%, "leaned" and "muttered" 82.1%; trigrams "voice barely whisper" 68.7%, "said voice low" 61.2%, "air thick scent" 49.3%, "took deep breath" 44.8%. ### Inferences
- Phrase candidates with large measured ratios and low FP in expository prose, none currently in PHRASES: `\bas a (poignant|powerful|stark|sobering) reminder\b`, `\breminder of the (enduring|potential)\b`, `\bfaced numerous challenges\b`, `\b(provide|provides|providing|offer|offers) new insights into\b`, `\bthe complex interplay\b`, `\b(a )?newfound sense of\b` and `\brenewed sense of purpose\b`, `\beven in the face of\b` (moderate FP), `\bblur(s|red|ring)? the lines? between\b`, `\bbecome a focal point\b`, `\bcan vary depending on\b`, `\b(fascinating|complex) and (complex|fascinating|nuanced)\b`, `\bcouldn'?t help but (feel|wonder|notice)\b`, `\ba (profound|deep) sense of\b`.
- Assistant-voice openers are measurable and absent from PHRASES: `^\s*(certainly|sure|absolutely)!\s*here'?s\b`, `\bhere'?s what you need to know\b`, `\bi'?d be happy to\b`, `\blet me know if you'?d\b`, `\byou'?re touching on\b`, `\bbased on the (text|information provided)\b` (Claude), `^\s*below is\b` (ChatGPT). The skill already bans offer-of-help closes (R5.13) by reading, but the linter has no regex for them.
- Fiction words (flickered, muttered, leaned, shimmered, stammered, Elara/Kael names, "voice barely a whisper", "took a deep breath", "air thick with") are high-ratio but common in human fiction; FP risk high outside a fiction profile.

### Gaps
- Pangram per-model-family phrase lists are not public.
- Sun et al. top-phrase figures (Figures 6, 7, 15) did not extract as text, so the full per-family lists were not captured.
- EQ-Bench per-model slop scores for GPT-5/6, Claude 4.x/5.x, Gemini 3.x were not retrieved.

## Q3. Tells tied to newer models (2025 to 2026)

### Takeaway
The strongest quantified new-model evidence is for Claude: "load-bearing" jumped about 24x from Opus 4.6 to 4.7 and stayed high in Opus 5, and a 544k-document GitHub PR corpus shows a rising word cluster built on load-bearing, vacuous, quietly, genuinely, plainly, survived, wedged, loudly, provably. A second rising cluster carries sits, kicker, lede, eyebrow, hairline and British spellings; a third (containing gpt-6 tokens) carries fail-closed, exact-head, non-claims, source-backed, bounded, durable, materially, conservatively. Cluster-to-model attribution is inferred, not labelled.

### Inferences
- Word candidates with quantified support for the 2025 to 2026 Claude register: load-bearing (strongest: per-model rates plus 848-account PR evidence), quietly, genuinely, plainly, vacuous/vacuously, loudly, provably, wedged, papered over, chokepoint, tripwire, backstop, carve-out, -shaped (as in "X-shaped"), sharpest, structurally, earns its keep. FP risk: load-bearing literal in construction and in "load-bearing wall"; quietly and genuinely common in human fiction and speech, so flag at density (2+ per document) or in technical register only; vacuous/provably/tripwire/backstop are legitimate jargon in testing and formal methods.
- "sits" and "kicker" have lift 8 to 9 in a rising PR cluster, so the user's suspicion has some quantified support; "lands", "surface" (verb), "vibes", "real" as intensifier, "honestly" did not appear in any quantified source found. Mark those as anecdotal only.
- British spelling (colour, centred, grey, behavioural, unrecognised, maths) in an otherwise US-English codebase or author is a new candidate signal from the rising clusters. It is not a lexical word list; it would be a document-level check, and the FP risk is high for any British author.
- The GPT-6-associated PR cluster vocabulary (fail-closed, non-claims, source-backed, evidence-only, exact-head, non-goals, receipts, materially, conservatively) is a candidate PR-register group, but it is attribution by cluster, not labelled data, and many tokens are project jargon.
- The load-bearing case shows a tell can be caused by the harness prompt rather than the model alone, so a tell's rate can drop abruptly when the vendor edits a system prompt.

### Gaps
- No quantified per-word study for GPT-5.x (2025) or Gemini 2.5/3.x prose found. GPT-6 and Opus 5.x evidence beyond load-bearing is anecdotal.
- The PR-corpus clusters are unlabelled; the "Claude" label is the author's inference from timing and vocabulary, not ground truth. Cluster membership and lifts change with each daily refit.
- The Washington Post 328K-message ChatGPT analysis (2025) was cited secondhand only; its word list and numbers were not verified.

## Q4. Older tells that have measurably faded (retire or demote)

### Takeaway
Measured fading exists for the 2023 ChatGPT words named in early-2024 press (delve, intricate, realm, showcasing, pivotal, commendable, meticulous, innovative, notable, versatile) on arXiv from about March to April 2024, and for GPT-4-era PR vocabulary (comprehensive, enhance, streamline, robust, seamless, enterprise-grade) whose cluster fell from 11.4% to 1.5% of PR descriptions in 2025 to 2026. Neither is evidence that the words stopped being tells, only that they are less common; demote, don't delete.

### Cited Findings
- Geng & Trotta (arXiv 2502.09606, Feb 2025), arXiv abstracts 2018 to 2024, per-10k-abstract monthly frequencies: words flagged as ChatGPT-overused began decreasing around March to April 2024: delve, and the Liang et al. sets (pivotal, intricate, realm, showcasing; commendable, innovative, meticulous, notable, versatile). "significant" and "additionally" continued to rise; their own earlier list (significant, crucial, effectively, additionally, comprehensive, enhance, capabilities, valuable) was the rising group. Interpretation: human-LLM "coevolution" (authors edit or select outputs). Source: [arXiv 2502.09606](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09606)
- Hubarava & Ineichen (Oct 2026): some lexical markers "rise and then recede" while others accelerate; no named words in the abstract. Source: [arXiv 2610.26008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26008)
- Load-bearing PR corpus: GPT-4-era cluster 8 (comprehensive, enhancing, streamlining, robust, seamless, scalability, enterprise-grade) fell from 11.4% to 1.5% of PR descriptions across Jan 2025 to Nov 2026; Jules-style UI cluster fell 7.9% to 3.2%. Source: [analysis.js](https://louisabraham.github.io/load-bearing/analysis.js)
- Counter-evidence: the Kobak group's 2026 full-text follow-up still uses delves among its 379 markers and finds the aggregate LLM-vocabulary signal at its highest yet in December 2025 (0.89 of PMC papers). Pangram (Nov 2026) still lists "delve into" at 45x. The critical review (Apr to Sep 2026) found no study reporting a word in absolute decline. Source: [arXiv 2608.10715](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10715); [Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/resources/most-common-ai-phrases); [arXiv 2604.19139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19139)
- Korean abstracts: LLM-marker rise flattens mid-2026 (plateau, not decline). Source: [arXiv 2609.07447](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07447)

### Inferences
- The fall in delve and its peers after spring 2024 measures human editing of LLM output as much as model change; a text that still contains them is still more likely LLM-touched, and older models stay in use. Keep them; if slop-lint adds tiers, mark the 0.1.0 FSU/PubMed group as "2023 to 2024 era, declining in academic corpora since Mar 2024 (Geng & Trotta)".
- In PR and commit register, the 0.1.0 marketing group (comprehensive, robust, seamless, enhance, streamline) now marks an older generation rather than current frontier output; current Claude/GPT-6-era PR text needs the Q3 vocabulary to be caught.
- No source supports retiring any specific entry outright.

### Gaps
- Geng & Trotta give no per-word numeric decline (charts only) and stop at end of 2024. No 2025 to 2026 English per-word trend for delve was found.

## Q5. Register-specific tells (commit messages, code comments, PR descriptions, emails, LinkedIn)

### Takeaway
PR descriptions are the only register with a large quantified 2025 to 2026 corpus (the load-bearing project, 544k descriptions); it supplies the Q3 clusters and the em-dash surge. Email and review registers have phrase lists from Pangram without per-phrase rates. No quantified lexical studies were found for commit messages, code comments, or LinkedIn posts.

### Cited Findings
- PR descriptions: rising clusters and lifts as in Q3; median description 65 words; em dash 0.2 to 123 per 10k words (early 2024 to mid 2026). Source: [load-bearing README](https://github.com/louisabraham/load-bearing)
- Commit messages: the #53454 reporter observed "load-bearing" in chat, documents, and commit messages (Opus 4.7, Claude Code 2.1.120); one project commit cited the issue while rewording LLM filler. Anecdotal, no rate. Source: [GitHub issue #53454](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/53454)
- Email/letters: Pangram's list includes "i am writing to provide", "you for your continued dedication", "let me know if you'd", "recently had the pleasure", "you or someone you know". Reviews: "highly recommend for anyone", "is a compelling read", "read for anyone interested", "its compact design", "noticeable lag", "i ordered their signature". No per-phrase rates. Source: [Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/resources/most-common-ai-phrases)
- Social replies: Pangram "you're touching on"; Sun et al. show Claude echoes the prompt ("based on the text"), consistent with slop-lint's validate-then-restate rules. Source: [Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/resources/most-common-ai-phrases); [arXiv 2502.12150](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150)
- Consultant register (anecdotal): pressure-test, right-size, north star, true north, strategic imperative, double-click on. Source: [claudisms.ai](https://claudisms.ai/)

### Inferences
- For PR and commit linting, a separate opt-in group is justified: load-bearing, vacuous, vacuously, provably, wedged, papered over, chokepoint, tripwire, carve-out, backstop, "earns its keep", "-shaped", quietly, genuinely, plainly, loudly (as in "fails loudly"), plus older-gen comprehensive/enhance/streamline/enterprise-grade/production-ready. FP risk is real for testing jargon (vacuous truth, mutation testing "survived", fail loudly).
- Email phrases ("I am writing to provide", "thank you for your continued dedication", "I recently had the pleasure") are cheap regexes with low FP in technical docs but moderate FP in actual business email.

### Gaps
- No quantified study of LLM vocabulary in commit messages, code comments, or LinkedIn posts was found in this pass. A LinkedIn AI-share study (Originality.ai, 2024) is known by reputation but was not retrieved, and it measured prevalence, not words.
