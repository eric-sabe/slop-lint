# Shift slop-lint's next catalogue toward structure

The biggest gains available to slop-lint now need new rule machinery, and the cheapest one fixes a gap in its own matching: `reWord()` matches whole words only, so about 50 inflections of catalogued stems ("delved", "underscored", "fostering", "harnessing") pass today, and many of them sit on the measured PubMed excess-vocabulary list ([Kobak et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016v5)). The best-measured new evidence is structural and needs document-level rates: instruction-tuned GPT-4o uses present participial clauses at **5.3 times the human rate** and nominalizations at 2.1 times ([Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)), LLM argumentative essays carry about 1.7 times the expert-human tricolon rate per sentence ([Bakhshi 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768)), and 8 of 9 models write flatter sentence-length rhythm than matched news ([Lanzo and Angelo](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/164820)). Practitioners in 2025 and 2026 converged on a "staging" register with three or more independent sightings per item ("quietly", "genuinely", "load-bearing", "let that sink in", "here's the kicker", "earns its keep", "This matters."), none of which the linter catches. Machine artifacts that humans never type (`oaicite`, `turn0search0`, `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, Gemini `[cite: N]`, "Regenerate response") deserve their own group at FAIL severity. The em-dash has moved to "historical" on Wikipedia and now mainly fingerprints Claude through Opus 5, so the FAIL should stay as house style while the "near-decisive" rationale goes. For the skills, no-ai-tells should absorb the staging moves and replace "one hedge max" with a functional definition of hedging, because measured academic LLM text under-uses calibrated hedges and over-uses performative ones. serious-academic gains the most: 2026 studies give numbers for centrality-claim introductions (58 to 84 percent of model introductions versus 46 percent of human ones), report-by-paper literature reviews, scope drift (LLM summaries 4.85 times more likely to overgeneralize), and generic peer reviews, and Gopen and Swan's stress-position test should replace its fixed 40-word cap. No source supports retiring any catalogue entry, and every regex sketch below is still unscreened against `corpus/baseline`.

## Every candidate below still needs the baseline screen

The five research notes (2026-11-25) measured coverage against `slop-lint.mjs` 0.9.1, the no-ai-tells SKILL.md (catalogue 0.9.0) and `corpus/specimens/PENDING.md`, and report only what those miss. Each row in the tables carries an evidence grade, a source, an independent-sighting count, a false-positive risk and an action. Sighting counts collapse shared lineages the way the practitioner note does: skills built on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" count as Wikipedia, Antislop and slop-score count once because Sam Paech built both, Jim Bennett's two posts are one study, and secondary reports of the LM Arena Opus 5 analysis or of The Economist count once.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| M1 | Measured: large corpus or controlled study with a quantified effect |
| M2 | Measured but weaker: one corpus, vendor counts, LLM-annotated, abstract-only, small n, or unlabelled clusters |
| P | Practitioner consensus: independent reports with no measurement |
| A | Anecdotal: one report, or secondhand only |

False-positive risk is Low when near-zero baseline hits are expected, Med when some human registers use the form and it needs a scope or a screen, and High when it is common in human prose and belongs in a density rule or an opt-in group. "Ship after screen" means the item meets the project's bar (two independent sightings for a structural mold, or a measured source for a word) and still needs zero hits on `corpus/baseline`.

The screen itself has a weakness the notes did not catch. In this checkout, all 21 gov-speeches files and all 20 MedlinePlus files are stored as a single line each, and only the 14 Wikinews files keep line breaks (checked with `wc -l` on 2026-11-25). The `^`-anchored reply rules were therefore screened against about 41 document starts, and any paragraph, heading, list-run or first-sentence rule proposed below has no paragraph structure to test against in two of the three registers. The baseline also has no casual typed prose ([CHANGELOG 0.8.0](../../CHANGELOG.md)), which matters for the email, social and reply candidates. Rebuilding the baseline with paragraph breaks preserved is a precondition for most of section two.

## New machinery carries most of the measured evidence

Most measured findings describe rates, positions or layouts that a per-line regex cannot express. `lintText` splits on newlines and has no sentence splitter, document counter or position scope. The cheapest path to calibrated thresholds is to prototype each rate in `corpus-stats.mjs`, which already counts em dashes, semicolons, curly quotes and bold per 1k words, compare `corpus/baseline` against `corpus/samples`, and only then move the rule into `lintText`. The published per-1k rates for Reinhart's features sit in PNAS SI Tables S5 and S6, which were unreachable, but the paper's public feature file (`hap-e-biber.csv.gz`) reproduces its ratios: present participial clauses run 1.73 per 1k words for humans against 9.09 for GPT-4o, and nominalizations 14.6 against 31.3 (see `2026-11-verification-academic.md`). Those feature definitions differ from the linter's regexes, so thresholds still have to come from the repo's own corpora.

Some rate rules should not be built at all. Humans used assertive rhetorical questions at more than twice the LLM rate (5.55 versus 2.28 per document), so a question-density rule would point the wrong way ([Bakhshi](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19768)). Agentless-passive under-use (about 0.5x) is real but absence is weak evidence. Downtoners and clausal coordination flip sign between GPT-4o and Llama 3, so any universal rule would misfire on one family ([Reinhart](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)). Second-person density depends on register. The function-word skeleton that approximates Shaib's syntactic templates is plausible, since 76% of templates in model text occur in pretraining data versus 35% for human text and RLHF does not remove them ([Shaib et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00211)), but the approximation itself is untested and belongs in an experimental flag. The ceiling for all of this is modest: syntax-only features reached an AUROC of 0.64 to 0.68 for a human "slop" label, while LLM judges agreed with humans at kappa near zero ([Shaib et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19163v3)). That supports slop-lint's design choice of warnings over verdicts.

## Calibration: the em-dash now mostly fingerprints Claude through Opus 5

The em-dash rule is the one place where outside evidence moved against the tool's stated rationale. Wikipedia's AI Cleanup page now files em-dash overuse under "Historical indicators (2022-September 2026)" and says newer models are tuned to suppress it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). The Economist's July 2026 count of 55,940 sentences found that only Claude used more em dashes than human writers, with ChatGPT using markedly fewer ([Daring Fireball quoting The Economist](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/08/11/economist-ai-writing)). Bennett measured Claude Opus 5 at 12.9 per 1,000 words and Opus 5.5 at two in 57,000 words ([Arize](https://arize.com/blog/anthropic-says-it-fixed-claudes-writing/)). Some registers still carry a high rate: GitHub PR descriptions went from 0.2 to 123 em dashes per 10,000 words between early 2024 and mid-2026 ([Abraham PR corpus](https://github.com/louisabraham/load-bearing)), and Pangram's November 2026 table puts OpenAI output at 45 and Anthropic at 32 per 10,000 words against 5 for humans, with Gemini 3 Pro at 3 ([Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/resources/most-common-ai-phrases)). The repo's own corpus agrees with The Economist: `node corpus-stats.mjs` on this checkout gives 7.4 em dashes per 1k words for the Claude samples and 7.1 for Grok, but 4.6 for Gemini and 2.1 for GPT, against **4.2 for the human baseline**. The FAIL is still defensible as house style, and slop-lint exists to enforce it, but the header comment ("The one near-decisive typographic tell") and README ("the single most reliable signal") now overstate it. Suggested wording: "FAIL (exit 1): the em-dash (U+2014), as a house-style rule. As an authorship signal it is strongest for Claude through Opus 5 and for older OpenAI output. Newer models suppress it, so a clean result says nothing about authorship."

## serious-academic: turn the 2025 and 2026 academic studies into tests

The professor's two complaints in the skill's origin note now have measured counterparts. "Lists of facts without connecting them" matches report-by-paper literature reviews and support-to-support paragraph transitions at about half the human rate. "Rebut a rejection that wasn't revealed" matches rebuttal-to-rebuttal transitions at 52.0% for default LLMs versus 32.4% for humans ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01736v1)), although that corpus is public debate, not research papers. The craft sources supply the tests. Their own evidence is worked revision, not experiment: the craft note found no controlled study of stress position or topic strings, and no study measuring these features in LLM academic text.

## Measured evidence clusters in academic and syntactic work

The measured record is strong in three areas. Academic vocabulary and prevalence rest on Kobak's 15 million abstracts and its 2026 full-text follow-up, Liang's conference-review and 1.1-million-paper studies, and Geng and Trotta's arXiv trends. Grammatical and rhetorical features rest on Reinhart's parallel corpora, the 2025 stylistic benchmark, Shaib's template and slop studies, and Bakhshi's LLM-annotated rhetoric counts. Academic argument and citation behavior rest on the CARS introduction study, Peters and Chin-Yee, Kim et al., the Jiang and Hyland metadiscourse series, Academ-AI and the citation-fabrication studies. Most of these predate the current frontier models: Reinhart tested GPT-4o and Llama 3, Kobak's word list is GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 era, and no Biber-style replication on GPT-5.x, Claude Opus 5.x or Gemini 3.x was found.

For 2025 and 2026 models, measurement is thin and mostly M2. The only per-model word rates are the community counts for "load-bearing" in issue #53454 and Bennett's 153-flag comparison of Opus 5 and Opus 5.5. The 544,271-description PR corpus is large but unlabelled, so its "Claude" and "GPT-6" clusters are the author's inference from timing and vocabulary, and the clusters change with each daily refit. Pangram publishes per-10k rates for a handful of phrases without describing its corpus. The staging moves (P24 to P42) are practitioner consensus with no frequency data, and two of the best-placed sources (Tech Xplore and PostHero) say outright that no study of "quietly" or "genuinely" exists.

Several items are anecdotal only and should not ship on current evidence: "lands", "surface" as a verb and "vibes" (no quantified source found), the Wikipedia GPT-6 word list (no on-wiki examples), Gemini 2.5 and 3 prose tells beyond citation markup, U+202F in GPT-5 output, the Barron's corporate-filing counts and the Washington Post 328,000-message figure (both secondhand), the Fable 5.1 guide wording (read only through quotes), and the claim that post-September 2026 models are "hard coded" against em dashes. "sits" and "kicker" do have M2 support (lifts of 8.6 and 8.7 in a rising PR cluster), which is more than the other suspected Claude words have.

## Open gaps block threshold-setting for the rate rules

The largest gap is calibration data. Reinhart's SI Tables S5 and S6 stayed unreachable, although the paper's public feature file gives per-1k-word means (participial clauses 1.73 for humans against 9.09 for GPT-4o, nominalizations 14.6 against 31.3), and no published human baseline exists for headings, bold spans or bullets per 1k words in comparable genres. Paragraph-length uniformity and sentence-opening variety have no published LLM-versus-human number at all. Lanzo and Angelo was read at abstract level, so per-model burstiness values are unknown. Until those numbers exist, every M8 threshold has to come from the repo's own corpora, and two of the three baseline registers have no paragraph structure (section one).

Model coverage is the second gap. No per-word 2025 or 2026 English excess ranking was found (the Kobak group's code at kobaklab/llm-usage-in-pmc was not checked), no quantified study covers GPT-5.x or Gemini 2.5 and 3 prose, and Pangram's per-family phrase lists are private. Juzek and Ward's 21 focal words did not extract, Sun et al.'s per-family phrase figures did not extract, and EQ-Bench slop scores for current models were not retrieved.

Register coverage is the third. No quantified study exists for commit messages, code comments or LinkedIn vocabulary, and the email tells come only from detector vendors. No study counts closing offers, "Great question" openers or second-person rates in assistant replies, and "uniform warmth" has never been measured, though LMArena's sentiment control shows it can be.

The academic side has its own holes. No study isolates Claude-family models on academic metadiscourse, no quantified study covers methods sections, limitation boilerplate, conclusion recaps or humanities monographs, and the argument-collapse evidence comes from public debate. The craft principles behind S1 to S6 have no experimental test, Hyland's per-discipline hedge norms were not accessed, only Sword's 5% nominalization threshold was confirmed, and the Williams and Pinker material came through handouts and summaries, so their wording needs checking against the books before it enters the skill. On the practitioner side, the DeepSeek bracket pattern and the ChatGPT PUA range are inferred, the El Attar et al. paper cited by humanizer was not verified, and the Wikipedia talk page was not reviewed for proposed tells.

## Conclusion

The evidence now splits cleanly by how fast a tell can be patched. Vocabulary and typography change within a release cycle: GPT-5.1 was tuned off the em-dash, Opus 5.5 cut it to near zero, and "load-bearing" traced partly to a client's built-in prompt that a vendor can edit in an afternoon. Structure is slower to move. Instruction tuning drives the participial and nominal style, larger models do not remove it, RLHF leaves syntactic templates in place, and an explicit instruction to vary sentences does not restore human rhythm. A linter that wants to stay useful past the next release should therefore spend its next versions on scopes and rates, and treat word lists as an era-labelled archive that keeps catching older models.

The three tools also need one shared definition they currently lack. no-ai-tells caps hedges, serious-academic sizes claims, and the linter flags hedge phrases, while the measured academic record says LLM drafts carry too few calibrated hedges and too many performed ones. A hedge defined by its function (attached to an interpretation, matched to the evidence, never stacked, never on a measured value) lets all three agree, and it is the change most likely to improve the academic drafts that started serious-academic.
